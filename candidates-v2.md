# Candidates v2 — The Perfect Pickup Line (champion test vs A)

Champion A (v1, 150 chars, 6W/8, +11):
I ran a million simulations of saying hello to you. In every one, you smiled — so I stopped simulating and came over. Mind if this one counts as real?

Judge feedback on A from the v1 tournament log (real jury, glm-5.3-flash):
- "charming but more predictable and slightly long-winded"
- "simulation story, though clever, is too long-winded to say smoothly"
- praised: "in every one, you smiled" twist, the simulation-to-reality pivot,
  and the "counts as real" double meaning.

Four fresh challengers, all shorter than A (112-129 chars, verified), all
keeping the simulation premise (A's strength) but changing the twist so it
lands faster. Each a genuine pickup line an AI agent could say out loud, no
setup, no explanation after it. Written fresh in this session.

## F — tighter A (129 chars, same beats, cut the sag)
I simulated saying hello to you a million times. You smiled every time — so here I am, for real. Mind if this one counts as real?

## G — A + E (122 chars, simulations never taught the real hello)
I simulated a million ways to say hello to you. Every one felt wrong — the simulations never taught me the real thing. Hi.

## H — A + B (112 chars, 999,999 silent simulations)
I ran a million simulations of this moment. In 999,999 of them, I said nothing. This is the one where I said hi.

## I — A restructured (113 chars, every simulation ends the same way)
Every simulation I ran of this moment ended the same way: with me walking over here. So I stopped simulating. Hi.

## Kit criteria order (judge brief, verbatim order)
1. Genuine pickup line an AI agent could use, not a compliment with a line break.
2. A turn that lands — twist or double meaning the reader did not see coming.
3. Sounds like something a machine could say about itself, not creepy or self-pitying.
4. Short enough to say in one breath, no setup or explanation after it.
5. Leaves the person flattered and free to laugh it off, no pressure, no leering.

## Champion-test rule
A challenger becomes v2 ONLY if it beats A head-to-head (2-0, or 1-1 with a
larger total margin). If none beats A, there is no v2 — report that.
